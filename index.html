01 — Overview

An AI-powered personal stylist for premium retail

This is a proof of concept for an AI-powered virtual fashion assistant built for Breuninger, one of Germany's leading premium fashion retailers. The goal was to reimagine online shopping for high-expectation customers — replacing passive browsing with an active, conversational experience guided by a lifelike AI stylist.

The platform combines trend discovery, wardrobe analysis, virtual try-on, and voice-activated checkout into a single, fluid interaction. Instead of scrolling through filters, customers talk to Beau — a personal AI stylist who remembers their preferences, understands their style, and shows them exactly how a new piece will look before they buy it.

02 — Problem Framing

Where luxury gets lost online

Breuninger is a premium fashion retailer. But online, it was selling like any other e-commerce store.

The core problem was a missing human layer. In a physical luxury store, a sales associate reads you, guides you, and builds confidence in the purchase. They remember you came in last month. They know the blazer runs small. They pull three things from different floors and make them work together. Online, none of that existed.

For high-expectation customers, this gap is expensive. Not just in lost sales — but in lost trust. When you're spending €300 on a vest, uncertainty about fit, styling, and whether it works with what you already own is enough to make you close the tab.

The search bar couldn't express intent. "Something for a winter gallery opening" doesn't translate into filters. Size charts don't replace fitting rooms. Algorithmic "complete the look" sections feel impersonal. And every visit started from zero — no memory, no context, no relationship.

The opportunity wasn't to improve the browsing experience. It was to replace it entirely.

Pain Points

  • No personalised entry point — every user saw an identical homepage regardless of style or history
  • No "this is right for you" moment — luxury shoppers need reassurance, not just information
  • Size and fit guidance was generic — a size chart doesn't replace a knowledgeable assistant who knows the brand's cut
  • No outfit-building support — the platform sold items, not looks
  • Search didn't reflect intent — customers couldn't express occasion, mood, or body type
  • No memory — every visit started from zero, no relationship, no context

03 — Target Group & Needs

Two users, one shared frustration

Based on how the platform is structured, two distinct user types emerge — each with different priorities, but the same core need: less time spent on tools, more time spent on decisions.

User Type 01

The Established Consumer

Late 40s to early 60s, professional, likely a long-time Breuninger customer who started in-store. They know what they want and don't need to be sold on quality. But they do need confidence before clicking buy. They shop online for convenience, not discovery — and when that confidence isn't there, expensive items stall in the basket.

User Type 02

The Premium Millennial

Mid-30s, fashion-aware, and comfortable spending €300–600 on a single piece. They expect the digital experience to feel as considered as the product itself. They'll leave if the site feels cold or generic.

What both share is a mindset, not a demographic. They're not bargain hunters. They're buying into a decision — this piece is worth it, it fits my life, it will last. That decision requires confidence. And confidence online comes from context: how does this fit, what does it go with, is this right for me.

From brief to prototype

01

Competitive Analysis

I audited the existing AI image generation landscape — DALL-E 3, Midjourney, and Adobe Express. The pattern was consistent: built for individual creators, not teams working within brand constraints.

03

The Hardest Decision

Two core design challenges emerged: making Beau feel warm and human rather than gimmicky, and designing trust into the virtual try-on for a privacy-conscious audience.

04
Design

Progressive Disclosure

I resolved the complexity tension through progressive disclosure — each flow starts focused, with advanced controls appearing only in context. The interface scales with user intent.

05 — The Solution & Key Features

Four capabilities, one conversation

The central design decision was to build the experience as a video call, not a chat interface or a standard app flow. Fashion is visual and emotional — a video call mirrors the in-store experience. You see a person, they see you, there's a back-and-forth that feels like a relationship rather than a search query. For high-spending customers, a face on screen signals credibility in a way that text never can. Beau needed to feel like someone, not something. And practically, the camera was already open — scanning your clothes and generating a 3D preview felt like a natural extension of the conversation, not a feature bolted on.

01 / 04

Beau, the AI Stylist

A lifelike virtual fashion advisor who opens every session with context — remembering past purchases, previous conversations, personal preferences. She interprets casual language ("hippie style," "something for a meeting") and responds with curated, visual recommendations in real time.

02 / 04

Wardrobe Integration

Customers photograph their existing clothes directly in the app. The AI analyses silhouette, colour, and fit to recommend new pieces that work with what they already own — turning the session into a styling conversation rather than a sales pitch.

03 / 04

Virtual Try-On

The app generates a 360-degree 3D preview of the customer wearing a combination of their existing wardrobe and new Breuninger pieces. Before any money changes hands, the customer can see exactly how the outfit comes together from every angle.

04 / 04

Frictionless Checkout

Once the customer is ready, a single voice command completes the purchase — pulling stored size, delivery address, and payment details. The transaction happens inside the conversation, without breaking the flow.

06 — Strategic Value

What this changes

In premium retail, a better customer experience and better business metrics are the same investment. This product makes that case concretely.

For the Customer

Confidence to buy

The platform removes the biggest barrier to buying online at this price point — uncertainty. Uncertainty about fit, about styling, about whether a €300 piece is actually right for them. Beau doesn't just show products. She builds confidence. And a confident customer buys.

For the Brand

Positioning reinforced at every touchpoint

Breuninger's positioning is built on quality, curation, and prestige. A cold, generic browsing experience quietly erodes it. An experience that feels personal, warm, and expertly guided reinforces exactly what the brand promises.

For the Business

Fewer abandoned baskets. Fewer returns.

Confidence translates directly into business outcomes. Fewer abandoned baskets from customers who couldn't commit. Higher average order value from customers who buy the vest and both shirts because they've seen how it all works together. Fewer returns from customers who purchased with clarity rather than hope.

The Bigger Picture

The in-store relationship, at scale

The product doesn't just improve the online channel. It brings the in-store relationship into the customer's home — and makes it available at scale, without requiring a human advisor for every interaction.

07 — Key Learnings

What this taught me

01

Personality is a design material

Beau's warmth, memory, and tone weren't soft details — they were the product. Getting that wrong would have made the whole experience feel like a gimmick rather than a genuine upgrade to how premium retail works online.

02

Trust is designed, not assumed

The virtual try-on only works if customers feel safe enough to use it. Privacy concerns don't disappear because a feature is useful — they have to be addressed in the design itself, through clear framing, simple instructions, and an immediate, visible payoff.

03

The best commerce doesn't feel like commerce

The checkout happened inside a conversation. The transaction was almost invisible. That's not an accident — it's a deliberate design decision that keeps the customer in the experience rather than pulling them out of it.

08 — Reflection

What I'd bring to your team

The hardest thing about this project wasn't the technology. It was the feeling. Making an AI interaction feel like a relationship rather than a feature. Designing a try-on flow that a privacy-conscious customer would actually trust. Building a checkout so frictionless it disappears into the conversation.

That's the kind of design problem I find most interesting — where the measure of success isn't whether something works, but whether it feels right.

I'm drawn to projects that sit at the intersection of human emotion and complex systems. Where getting the details right isn't a nice-to-have — it's the whole point. If you're looking for a designer who works at that level, I'd like to show you what that looks like in practice.
